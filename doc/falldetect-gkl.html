What this is

This is not a fall detector. It is an escalation system whose sensor happens to be an accelerometer. The firmware is perhaps 30% of the risk; the rest is whether a caregiver is actually told, and whether they believe it when they are.

The constraint that shapes everything

Detecting a fall is easy. Not firing on sitting down heavily, stairs, or leaning on a walking frame is the engineering problem. One false alarm at 3 am and the device comes off permanently â€” and then you have nothing. Specificity, not sensitivity, is the budget.

Disclose this, don't discover it

v1 is an in-home system. A fall in the garden, the street or a shop will not be reported. That is a deliberate, bounded scope â€” but it must be stated plainly to the wearer and their family. If coverage anywhere is genuinely required, the XIAO is the wrong board and an nRF9160 / LTE-M is the answer.

The firmware rule that keeps the door open: the wearable never knows what the gateway is. It advertises, connects, emits events, retries until acknowledged. Adding a phone as a second path later becomes a gateway and cloud change with zero firmware impact.

Decisions

IDDecisionChosenConsequence
D1ConnectivityFixed home hub Reliable indoors, zero outdoor coverage. Phone path is additive in v2.
D2Mount pointWrist Best compliance, worst accuracy. Moves risk from the product into the firmware.
D3ToolchainZephyr / nRF Connect SDK Upstream board support, real power management, MCUboot OTA.

D2 is the consequential one

Wrist was chosen for compliance, and that is a defensible trade â€” a worn device with a weaker algorithm beats an accurate device in a drawer. But the arm moves independently of the body, so two of the four classic cascade stages degrade, and the wrist sees far more high-g daily activity than the trunk ever does.

There is also a wrist-specific complication worth designing around: the fall may land on the arm wearing the device (huge spike, arm pinned under the body) or on the other side (muted signal, arm free). These look completely different, and the dataset must contain both.

Gyro-on-demand â€” the idea that makes wrist viable

Angular velocity carries much of the fall signature at the wrist. But the gyro draws milliamps against tens of microamps for the accelerometer, so running it continuously destroys the battery target outright. The resolution is that you never have to:

Because the IMU's FIFO has already been batching accelerometer data, this also recovers the pre-trigger samples â€” the free-fall phase that happened before the MCU woke, which a naive wake-on-interrupt design throws away.

What must not trigger it

The confounder list is longer at the wrist than anywhere else, which is precisely why S2 is hard here: clapping Â· waving Â· gesturing while talking Â· brushing teeth Â· washing hands Â· putting a hand down hard on a table Â· pushing up out of a chair by the armrests Â· taking the watch off and dropping it Â· using a walking stick or frame Â· leaning weight on the arm Â· reaching to a high shelf.

The one that will bite

Walking sticks and frames put high-g impact loading straight through the wrist, hundreds of times a day, in exactly the users most likely to fall. If the detector cannot separate a walking frame from a fall, S2 is unreachable. It needs its own dataset category.

And the fall that does not look like one: the slow slump, sliding down a wall or out of a chair. Little or no free-fall phase, so stage 1 never fires. Documented as a known v1 gap rather than papered over.

Two things here are not optional

PRE_ALERT goes out before the countdown resolves. If the device is smashed, the battery knocked loose, or the wearer crawls out of BLE range, the cloud has still heard something. The cloud rule is that a pre-alert followed by silence is a fall. Waiting for confirmation before transmitting means the worst falls are exactly the ones you fail to report.

The 30-second cancel window is what makes S2 survivable. It does not reduce the false-positive rate; it collapses the cost from "caregiver panic at 3 am" to "annoying beep the wearer silences" â€” and that is what determines whether the device stays worn. The wrist choice pays off here: the button is always reachable with the opposite hand.

Why tools/build.ps1 exists

cmake, ninja, dtc and the ARM compiler are not on PATH â€” they live only inside the toolchain bundle, so a bare west build in a fresh shell fails. The script sets the environment every time, so a build never depends on a hand-configured shell.

.\tools\build.ps1                    # product firmware at repo root
.\tools\build.ps1 blink -Pristine    # after prj.conf / overlay edits
.\tools\build.ps1 mic_record -Flash  # waits 60 s â€” double-tap RST

Flashing is a two-step dance: run with -Flash, then double-tap RST (two firm presses about a quarter-second apart). The XIAO-SENSE drive appears, the image is copied, and the board reboots into it automatically.

Safety and regulatory

This is a safety-adjacent device. It is not a certified medical device and must not be presented as a replacement for a professionally monitored medical alert system. Any real deployment needs explicit consent from the wearer, a clear statement of coverage limits, and a documented fallback when the system fails.
